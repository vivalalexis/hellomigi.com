# Migi Privacy Policy

Last updated 19 November 2026

Migi ("Migi: Everyday Japanese" on the App Store) helps you pick up everyday Japanese on your
iPhone, its widgets and your Apple Watch. You sign in so your progress follows you to any
device. This policy explains what we collect, why, who helps us handle it, how long we keep it
and the choices you have.

## Who we are

Migi is made by Shuyan Trisha Kuek, an individual developer based in Australia ("we", "us"). For
anything about privacy, email **tk@hellomigi.com**.

## The short version

- You sign in with Apple, Google or a 6-digit code sent to your email. There are no passwords.
- Your account and learning progress are stored in our database in **Sydney, Australia**, so
  they sync between your devices. Migi doesn't use iCloud.
- The app sends **usage statistics linked to your account** to help us tell whether Migi helps
  people learn. You can switch them off at any time. Where the EU/EEA rules apply, they're off
  unless you switch them on.
- Widgets and the Apple Watch app never connect to the internet. Say it listens on your iPhone
  only; your voice is never recorded to a file or sent anywhere.
- No ads, no tracking across other companies' apps or websites, and we never sell your
  information.
- You can delete your account and your learning progress from the app at any time.

## What we collect

**Your account**

- **Your email address.** If you sign in with Apple and choose "Hide My Email", this is a
  private relay address from Apple, and we never see your real one.
- **How you sign in** (Apple, Google or email), and an account ID our database creates for you.
- **If you sign in with Google:** Google also gives our sign-in service your **name** and a link
  to your **profile photo**, which are stored with your account. Migi doesn't use them today; we
  may use them later to personalise your profile in the app and the emails we send you. Sign in
  with Apple asks only for your email address.
- **Your Migi Plus plan:** which plan you have (annual, monthly, lifetime or a 30-day pass) and
  when it renews or ends.
- **Whether you've asked for news and tips by email**, and when you last changed that choice.
- **Sign-in records:** our sign-in service (Supabase) keeps a security log of sign-ins and
  sign-outs, including the IP address each came from.

**Your learning progress**

A history of what happens as you learn: which Japanese items the app has shown you and where
(app, widget, notification or Watch), your quiz answers, audio you play, words you save, hide or
mark as known, lessons you start and finish, the result of each Say it try (recognised or not,
never what you said), and any problem you report about an item (one of three set reasons). We
also keep your settings, including the "Where will you use Japanese?" topics you choose. This
history decides what the app shows you next. It's kept on your iPhone and in your account, so it
syncs between your devices and comes back when you sign in on a new one. Each entry carries a
random ID for the device it came from, which isn't linked to anything outside Migi.

**Usage statistics** (see below), if they're switched on.

We don't collect your contacts, your location, photos, recordings of your voice, or anything you
type into the app other than your email address and sign-in code.

## How we use it

- **To run Migi:** sign you in, keep your progress in sync, and know whether you have Plus.
- **To send emails you need:** your sign-in code. We send nothing else unless you ask.
- **To send news and tips:** only if you tick "Send me occasional news and tips" when you sign
  in, or switch it on later in Settings. It's unticked to start with. Every such email has an
  unsubscribe link, and you can switch it off in Settings at any time.
- **To keep Migi secure:** the sign-in log helps us spot misuse.
- **To improve Migi:** through usage statistics, if they're on.

## Usage statistics

**What's sent:** short, structured signals about how the app is used. Each one is an event name
and a few values from this list, and nothing else:

- which Japanese item it was about (an ID from our own content, such as *word.oishii*), and where
  (the app, a home-screen or Lock Screen widget, a notification, or the Watch app or
  complication);
- what you did with it: opened it, played audio, saved or unsaved it, hid it, marked it as
  known, brought it back, asked for another word, swiped a flashcard as known or missed, or
  the result of a Say it try;
- quiz results: the kind of quiz, how many choices it had, whether the answer was right, wrong
  or "don't know", roughly how long since the item was last shown (a range such as "1 to 3
  days"), and the scheduler's estimate as a range;
- practice rounds: the kind of round, how many items, and a duration range;
- lessons started and finished, which screens you visit, and which onboarding steps you reach;
- a daily count of items shown per surface, and your Progress counts (Shown, Getting it,
  Learned, Learned both ways);
- which setting you changed, and the new value for display and study settings only (such as
  romaji, furigana, audio speed, appearance or app icon);
- widgets you add (size and place), when the Plus sheet is shown, purchases (which plan), and
  when the app asks iOS to show a rating prompt;
- the item ID when you report a problem, without the reason.

Our analytics provider's software also adds technical details to each signal: app version, iOS
version, device model, screen size and orientation, language, region and time-zone settings,
light or dark mode, accessibility settings (such as text size and reduce motion), and the date
and hour. It also sends a signal when a new app session starts.

**What's never sent:** your email address, your name, your "Where will you use Japanese?"
topics, the reason you give when you report a problem, anything you say to Say it, exact times,
or your device's ID.

**How it's linked to you:** the statistics are tied to your account ID after it has been
scrambled (hashed) with a fixed secret value on your iPhone. Our analytics provider,
[TelemetryDeck](https://telemetrydeck.com), scrambles it again with its own secret on its
servers, so neither of us can turn it back into your account ID. On your devices, only the
iPhone app sends statistics; what widgets and the Watch record is sent by the iPhone app.
Nothing is sent before you sign in or while you're signed out.

**Your choice:**

- **EU and EEA:** if your App Store country (or, when it can't be read, your iPhone's region) is
  in the EU or EEA, statistics are **off** unless you choose "Share usage stats" during setup or
  in Settings.
- **Everywhere else:** statistics are **on** to start with, and you can switch them off at any
  time in **Settings → Share usage stats**.
- When they're off, the analytics software isn't started and nothing is sent, including
  anything the widgets or the Watch recorded while they were off. The choice applies to the
  iPhone you make it on.

## Purchases

Migi Plus, including its free trial, is sold by Apple. We never see your payment details. Each
purchase carries your Migi account ID, so Apple can tell our server which account it belongs to
and when it renews or ends. Your plan then shows on any device you sign in on. Apple also gives
us sales reports that don't identify you.

## Crash reports

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in iOS (Settings → Privacy & Security →
Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share crash reports with us. We use them only to fix bugs.

## What stays on your devices

- **Widgets** read what the Migi app saved on your iPhone. They never connect to the internet
  and never see your sign-in.
- **The Apple Watch app** gets a few days of words from your iPhone through Apple's
  phone-to-watch connection, and passes what you do back to the iPhone. It never connects to our
  servers or our analytics provider.
- **Notifications** are scheduled on your iPhone. We don't use a push notification service.
- **Say it** uses Apple's speech recognition on your iPhone (iOS 26 or later). Your iPhone asks
  for microphone permission first. The audio goes straight from the microphone to the
  recogniser; it's never saved to a file and never leaves your iPhone. Apple may download its
  Japanese speech model to your iPhone the first time. Only the result is kept, as above.
- **Your sign-in** is kept in your iPhone's Keychain.

## On our website

hellomigi.com uses TelemetryDeck's web script on its home page. Each time the page loads, the
script sends TelemetryDeck:

- the page address, including any campaign tags in the link (such as "ref");
- the address of the site you came from, if any;
- your browser's language setting;
- the website's ID in TelemetryDeck, the script's version number and a flag that marks test
  traffic.

Like any web request, it also carries your IP address and your browser's user agent (a line of
text that names the browser and operating system). The script sets no cookies and stores nothing
on your device.

TelemetryDeck says it uses the IP address and user agent to work out the browser, operating
system, device type and country. It also says it counts visitors by a scrambled code (a SHA-256
hash) made from the IP address, the website's ID in TelemetryDeck, the user agent and a value that
changes every day, so a visitor can't be recognised from one day to the next or across sites.
TelemetryDeck says it does no fingerprinting.

## International transfers

Your account and progress stay in Australia. Your email address is processed in Japan, and by a
US company, when we send you email. Usage statistics are processed in the EU. Apple and Google
handle their sign-in services under their own safeguards.

In version 1.0, Migi isn't sold in App Store countries in the EU, the EEA or the UK.

## How long we keep it

- **Your account, progress, settings and plan:** until you delete your account. Deleting it
  removes them from our database straight away. Copies may remain in our database backups, kept
  in Australia, for up to 30 days.
- **Sign-in log:** Supabase keeps it in its logs for up to 7 days. Deleting your account doesn't
  clear it sooner.
- **News and tips:** until you switch them off, unsubscribe or delete your account.
- **Signing out** erases Migi's copy on that iPhone, after syncing it one last time. Your
  account keeps everything until you sign in again.
- **Without Plus**, nothing is deleted: your account and progress stay until you delete them,
  and come back if you subscribe again.
- **Usage statistics:** TelemetryDeck keeps them. On our current plan we can view the last 3
  months; TelemetryDeck moves older statistics to storage we can't view, rather than deleting
  them on a set date. Because the account ID is scrambled twice, we can't pick out one person's
  statistics, so they aren't removed when you delete your account.
- **Website page views:** TelemetryDeck keeps them in the same way as usage statistics, above.

## Deleting your account

In the app, go to **Settings → Account → Delete account** (you need to be online). This
permanently deletes your account, your learning progress and your settings on all devices, and
removes your address from our news and tips list. If you signed in with Apple, Apple asks you
to confirm, and we cancel Migi's access to your Apple sign-in. If any step fails, nothing is
deleted and you can try again. Deleting your account doesn't cancel an App Store subscription;
the same screen offers **Manage subscription** for that.

## Legal bases

- **Your account, progress and plan:** to provide the service you asked for.
- **Sign-in log:** our legitimate interest in keeping accounts secure.
- **News and tips emails:** your consent, which you can withdraw at any time.
- **Usage statistics:** your **consent** where the EU/EEA rules apply; elsewhere, our
  **legitimate interest** in understanding and improving the app, and you can object at any time
  by switching statistics off.
- **Website page views:** our legitimate interest in knowing how people find and use the website.

## Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us what we hold about you, ask for a copy, ask us to correct or
delete it, object to processing, or withdraw consent, by emailing tk@hellomigi.com. You can do
much of this yourself in the app: switch statistics off, switch off news and tips, or delete
your account.

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, please contact us first. You can
also complain to your privacy regulator: in Australia the
[Office of the Australian Information Commissioner](https://www.oaic.gov.au), or elsewhere your
local privacy or data protection authority.

## Security

Sign-in uses Apple, Google or a one-time email code, so there are no passwords to steal. Your
data travels over encrypted connections, and our database lets each account read and change only
its own records. The keys that unlock everyone's data stay on our server and are never in the
app.

## Children

Migi is made for adults and teenagers learning Japanese. It isn't directed at children under 16,
and we don't knowingly collect information from them.

## Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top and describe important changes in the
app's release notes.
